# Project Summary
For this project, I wanted to see multiple visualizations about how two-high safety usage has affected how NFL teams draft in the first two rounds. 

## First Visualization, NFL Safety Coverage Usage
My first visualization, seen below, is a line chart that shows how the usage of two-high safety defense has increased in the past decade, while the use of one-high safety has slowly decreased. Since the usage of two-high safety wasn't an officially tracked statistic until 2015, it's unfortunately not a very historical depiction of two-high shell use. Despite that, it's obvious that in 2019, after seeing what the defensive formation did for Vic Fangio's defense, NFL teams finally started to implement it in their own defense. 

<img width="1200" height="300" alt="nfl_safety_usage" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/32056954-4227-467e-af38-34001e997284" />

This is because two-high safety defense is incredible at negating big gain passing plays, and it forces NFL teams to rely on their run and short passing game, which wasn't really a factor for many teams. By showing two-high safety, it also consistently confuses the opposing quarterback, as one safety could be blitzing, which would lead it to truly be one-high safety. During this time period, NFL teams weren't consistently drafting "scrambling" or "dual-threat" quarterbacks, and instead opted to rely on field generals, or quarterbacks who felt more comfortable passing the ball, such as Peyton Manning, Tom Brady, Drew Brees, and Matthew Stafford.
